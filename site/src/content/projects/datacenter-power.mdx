<Section id="method" title="Method">

Six public sources, no key and no paywall among them.

The **physical** answer comes from EIA-860M, the preliminary monthly generator inventory. Every unit
on the Operating, Planned and Canceled sheets is read, its EIA technology string mapped to one of
fifteen families and its status code to one of five stages. An unmapped technology stops the run
rather than falling into "other", because EIA adding a row type is news. Puerto Rico ships on its own
sheets and is excluded, as a separate interconnection.

**Deferred retirements** are a diff between two editions twelve months apart. A unit that actually
retired is not a deferral; nor is one whose date moved earlier. Both are excluded, and the schema
enforces the second by refusing a non-positive deferral.

The **demand** answer comes from EIA-861M monthly retail sales, with EIA's own national roll-up used
rather than a sum of the states, and from the Short-Term Energy Outlook for the forecast. The two
agree to a decimal on the overlapping history, which is the check that both are being read right.

**Regional attribution** comes from the PJM load forecast: summer peak per zone from one workbook,
the large-load adjustment from another. The two workbooks spell zones differently and the second
contains sub-area rows under blank zone cells, so zones are normalised and classified before
anything is summed — the additive zones reconcile to the RTO total to within a rounding error, which
is the check on that classification.

The **contract** answer and the **cost** comparison are curated by hand, one row per source, under
rules that live in the data files themselves. A row may only quote a freely reachable page. A
capacity may only be what was disclosed, never inferred. Every row carries a caveat written for a
reader and a date on which somebody last opened the source; after ninety days the page greys it.

<Section id="limits" title="What this page cannot tell you">

<ul>
  <li><strong>It cannot measure data-center consumption.</strong> No public dataset meters it. Every
  data-center-specific number here is either an operator's published figure or a company's claim,
  and the sector series are proxies that are named as proxies.</li>
  <li><strong>Nameplate capacity is not energy.</strong> The additions chart is megawatts. Converting
  to megawatt-hours needs a capacity factor per family, which would let a modelling choice drive the
  headline, so the page does not do it.</li>
  <li><strong>Contracted megawatts are not additional megawatts.</strong> A power purchase agreement
  with an existing nuclear plant moves an attribute between buyers. It builds nothing.</li>
  <li><strong>Only PJM publishes a large-load adjustment.</strong> ERCOT and the Southeast are
  covered by retail sales, which is a weaker instrument, and conclusions there are correspondingly
  weaker.</li>
  <li><strong>The planned inventory is not a forecast.</strong> Most of it has no regulatory
  approval. The page reports the under-construction subset beside it for exactly this reason.</li>
  <li><strong>The path taxonomy has no bucket for fusion.</strong> Two real agreements — Microsoft
  with Helion and Google with Commonwealth Fusion — are absent from the contract table because
  fusion is neither a restart, an uprate, a small modular reactor nor a renewable, and bending one
  of those codes to fit would hide the gap rather than show it.</li>
</ul>

</Section>
