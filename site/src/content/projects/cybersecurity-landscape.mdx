<h3>For readers outside China</h3>

This note is background, not a finding: the pool records neither customers nor owners, and the points come from company reports and public law texts, not from this page's data.

<ul>
  <li><strong>Boxes and projects, not subscriptions.</strong> Many listed Chinese security vendors sell appliances and project work that is delivered and booked in lumps, so revenue is uneven through the year
  and a half-year growth rate says less than it does for a subscription company. That is a reason the panel above uses trailing revenue and year-on-year change rather than a forward multiple.</li>
  <li><strong>State buyers.</strong> Customers are often government bodies, state-owned enterprises, carriers and banks, and they buy on budget cycles. Revenue fell year on year at {chinaFacts.falling} of the {chinaFacts.n} listings,
  but the page holds no customer data and cannot say why.</li>
  <li><strong>Compliance drives demand.</strong> The Cybersecurity Law (amended with effect from the start of 2026, with higher fines and a new clause on AI), the Data Security Law and the Personal Information Protection Law require
  protections that customers buy to pass inspection, and the multi-level protection scheme sets the controls a system must have to be certified.</li>
  <li><strong>Ratings are almost all positive.</strong> East Money's {chinaFacts.emRatings} ratings in its {chinaFacts.emWindow} window are {chinaFacts.emBuy} buy and {chinaFacts.emAdd} add, and its feed leaves neutral, reduce and sell blank (the note under the ratings figure says how the page reads that). <p><strong>What was registered.</strong> The {facts.scoreboard.length} claims in the scoreboard, with their populations, thresholds, lists and dates, are section I of
{' '}<a href={plan.url}>docs/EVALUATION_PLAN.md</a>, written {day(facts.as_of)} and committed {day(facts.registered)}. The file as it stands today hashes to <code>{plan.short}</code> (<a href={plan.history}>history</a>). Each claim states what

<p><strong>Not blind.</strong> Several claims are partly retrospective: the multiples, growth rates, free-cash-flow margins and layers of the registered companies had been seen before the thresholds were written, and the weekly

<p><strong>How a claim is judged.</strong> The rules apply in a fixed order. If a stated precondition fails, the claim is undecidable. If a holds condition and a falsified condition are both met, it is undecidable. If a falsified

are percentile bootstraps that resample companies, with {num(reg.draws)} draws requested and a fixed seed, so a reading can be reproduced exactly.</p>

<p><strong>Expected outcomes.</strong> With so few companies, several claims will end undecidable, and the section says how likely that is for each, from a simulation of cross-sections shaped like the baseline.</p>
