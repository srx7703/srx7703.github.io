<h3>Company filings and the KPI ledger</h3>

Volumes are not in the structured XBRL data most of the time: payment volume, GMV and spend are printed in the

<Section id="method" title="Method">

<p><strong>Question.</strong> What does each kind of payments company keep from the money it moves, and how
concentrated is each business when share is measured inside that business against a denominator that is written
down? Every chart above answers a part of that question; this section says how each number is made.</p>

<p><strong>Universe.</strong> The universe is pinned: {counts.roster_core} core companies charted and
{' '}{counts.roster_table} listed for classification only, each placed in the business that earns most of its
revenue. Merchant acquiring and buy now, pay later get full share treatment; corporate cards and cross-border get
ledgers, pool rankings and private intervals; issuing infrastructure and stablecoins are reference rows. A company
that sells across segments is counted once, in its largest. Ownership is a field: an acquired company keeps its
history and gains a status, so the {counts.acquisition_deals ?? '—'} acquisitions in the event timeline
({counts.events_by_kind?.acquisition ?? '—'} signing and closing events) do
not silently delete anyone. The list lives in <code>pipelines/payments/config.py</code>, and a test fails if it
drifts from the registered text.</p>

<p><strong>Data.</strong> Curated rows come from company filings and releases on
{' '}<a href={S[0].url}>SEC EDGAR</a> and investor-relations pages, the Federal Reserve's
{' '}<a href={S[1].url}>FEDS Note on buy now, pay later</a>, Visa's interchange schedule and the FSB's
cross-border cost indicators; {referenceFiles} curated files hold them, each row with a link, an as-of date, a
verification tag and a qualifier. The scheduled sources call public keyless endpoints:
{' '}<code>{'api.npmjs.org/downloads/range/<start>:<end>/<package>'}</code> for npm,
{' '}<code>{'cdn.httparchive.org/v1/adoption'}</code> for the HTTP Archive Tech Report,
{' '}<code>{'boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/<token>/jobs'}</code> and
{' '}<code>{'api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/<token>'}</code> for job boards, and EDGAR's submissions and
full-text search endpoints for Form D. None of them has run on this build
({Object.values(ci).filter(Boolean).length} of {Object.keys(ci).length} scheduled sources on disk). The
<a href="#data-built">section on how the data is built</a> walks through each source.</p>

<p><strong>Cleaning.</strong> A curated row reaches a number only when it is chartable: tag V (read on the
primary page) or C (computed from V inputs), and never a press report or third-party estimate. Every figure keeps
its qualifier, and a derived figure inherits a qualifier by a fixed rule.<Sidenote>For each input, ask which way the
result moves when the input rises. A "more than" input that pushes the result up makes the result a lower bound;
if it pushes the result down, the result is an upper bound; "less than" is the mirror image. Any "about" input makes the
result "about". Bounds pointing opposite ways give no bound: take rates refuse such a ratio, other sums keep the
point value and drop the bound.</Sidenote> Fiscal periods keep their filed labels; a share pool aligns them to
calendar periods by their end date and refuses a fiscal quarter that does not end in March, June, September or
December. Annualised run rates are never divided by, or pooled with, period totals. Currencies are not converted:
a euro or Australian-dollar reporter is excluded from a US dollar pool with that reason.</p>

<p><strong>Take rate.</strong> Revenue over the company's own volume, for a numerator and denominator pinned per
company, only when both rows share the period, the as-of date, the currency and the basis
(<code>ledger.py</code>):</p>

<pre><code>{`r = q_div(q_scale(Num(float(n["value"]), n["qualifier"]), 100.0), Num(float(d["value"]), d["qualifier"]),
          strict=True)`}</code></pre>

<p>Each ratio carries a kind, gross take rate, net take rate or margin retention, and ratios of different kinds
are never compared. A pair that fails a condition becomes a refusal row with the reason:
{' '}{counts.take_rates} take rates computed, {counts.take_rates_refused} refused.</p>

<p><strong>Per hundred dollars.</strong> A percentage of volume and a dollar figure per hundred dollars are the
same number, so a rate sheet and a filed revenue line land in the same column. A fixed fee needs a ticket size, and
every book is quoted for a hundred-dollar ticket (<code>economics.py</code>):</p>

<pre><code>{`def per_100(pct_rate: float, fixed_usd: float, ticket: float = TICKET_USD) -> float:
    return pct_rate + fixed_usd * 100.0 / ticket`}</code></pre>

<p>Lines are added only within a single book and period. Network fees Visa does not publish are a gap line with
no value, so the card residual is what the acquirer and the network keep together, never acquirer margin.</p>

<p><strong>Share and concentration.</strong> A disclosed-pool share is a member's volume over the sum of the
volumes its layer's companies disclose for the same calendar period and definition; the HHI is the sum of
squared shares in percent, inside that pool only (<code>share.py</code>):</p>

<pre><code>{`value = r.value / total
hhi = sum((got[m.label].value / total * 100) ** 2 for m in members) if enough else None`}</code></pre>

<p>A pool with a single disclosing member is refused, since its share is total by construction. Private
companies are markers beside a pool, never members, because a stated private volume is not a filing and can
overlap a public member (Shopify Payments runs on Stripe). An official-denominator share is computed only where
scope, measure and period match the official total; every mismatch is a refusal row:
{' '}{counts.share_rows} share rows, {counts.share_refusals} of them refusals.</p>

<p><strong>Baselines.</strong> Several readings had already been seen while the plan was being written, and the

from today's reading is in the <a href="#books">sensitivity chart</a>.</p>

<p><strong>Grading a qualified number.</strong> <code>evaluate.py</code> grades each question exactly as written,
from the marts, and from nothing else. A reading becomes an interval: a stated or "about" figure is its own value,
a "more than" figure runs from its value up, a "less than" figure from its value down. A comparison holds only when
the whole interval satisfies it, fails only when none of it does, and otherwise cannot tell:</p>

<pre><code>{`if op == ">=":
    return True if lo >= threshold else (False if hi < threshold else None)`}</code></pre>

<p>A question that cannot tell on its reading is undecidable, not graded on a guess. A question whose inputs
are not in yet, such as a future filing, rate sheet or crawl, or a source that only exists after the scheduled
run, is collecting, with that reason.</p>
