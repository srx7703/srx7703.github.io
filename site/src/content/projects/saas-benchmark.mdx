<Section id="method" title="Method">
  <div class="prose">
    <p><strong>Question.</strong> Using nothing but audited filings, how do public software companies compare on growth, profitability and the Rule of 40, and how much of the "clean" benchmark you see on investor sites survives contact with raw XBRL?</p>
    <p><strong>Data.</strong> The SEC's XBRL companyfacts API for each company (10-K and 10-Q only). Every fact carries a period start, end, value, fiscal year, form and filing date. Facts are deduplicated on their period with the latest filing winning, so restatements flow through automatically.</p>
    <p><strong>Tag mapping.</strong> Companies tag the same line item differently. Revenue alone appears under {Object.keys(tagCounts).length} tag combinations in this universe ({Object.entries(tagCounts).map(([k, v]) => `${v} × ${k}`).join('; ')}). Each metric has an ordered fallback list; facts from every tag are merged and the higher-priority tag wins on identical periods, which keeps full history for companies that switched tags.</p>
    <p><strong>Quarterly values.</strong> A fact spanning one fiscal quarter (80–100 days) is used directly. Otherwise consecutive year-to-date spans with the same start are differenced: 6M − 3M gives Q2, 9M − 6M gives Q3, FY − 9M gives Q4. That is how cash-flow items, which filers report year-to-date only, become quarterly, and how Q4 is recovered from the 10-K. Weighted-average share counts are averages, not flows, and are never differenced.</p>
    <p><strong>Metrics.</strong> All ratios use trailing-twelve-month sums of four consecutive quarters. Growth compares the TTM to the TTM one year earlier. FCF = operating cash flow − purchases of PP&amp;E − capitalised software. Rule of 40 = revenue growth (%) + FCF margin (%).</p>
  </div>
</Section>

<Section id="evaluation" title="Evaluation and limitations">
  <div class="prose">
    <p><strong>Reconciliation.</strong> Where a quarter is reported directly and can also be derived by differencing, the two are compared: {num(facts.reconciliation.agree)} of {num(facts.reconciliation.pairs)} pairs agree within {facts.reconciliation.tolerance} ({pct(facts.reconciliation.rate, 1)}). Disagreements are almost all quarters restated in a later filing, where the derived value comes from the restated year-to-date figure and the direct value from the original 10-Q; the direct value is kept and the pair is flagged.</p>
    <p><strong>Coverage.</strong> {facts.quarters_total} company-quarters of revenue. {Object.keys(facts.missing_latest).length === 0 ? 'Every company has revenue, gross profit, operating income, operating cash flow, capex and stock compensation in its latest quarter.' : `${Object.keys(facts.missing_latest).length} of ${facts.companies} companies lack a core metric in their latest quarter: ${Object.entries(facts.missing_latest).map(([t, ms]) => `${t} (${ms.join(', ')})`).join('; ')}. Gross profit is missing where the filer tags neither GrossProfit nor a cost-of-revenue line in the fallback list; capex is missing where the filer uses a tag outside the list. Both are coverage gaps, not zeros.`}</p>
    <p><strong>Limitations.</strong> Companies with a fiscal year ending in January (many enterprise-software names) report a quarter later than calendar-year filers, so "latest quarter" differs by up to two months across the table. Capitalised software is netted from FCF only where the company tags it. Foreign private issuers filing 20-F under IFRS are excluded, which is why Shopify is the only non-US name and monday.com is absent.</p>
  </div>
</Section>
