<Section id="method" title="Method">

**Revenue.** For each 10-Q and 10-K with a report date from {facts.data.first_report.slice(0, 4)} on (which also carry the year before as a comparison), the pipeline reads the filing's XBRL and keeps the revenue facts.
A series is a rule: one company, one revenue concept, and either no breakdown (a total) or one product line. When a company
restates a quarter, the latest filing wins. A three-month figure is used directly. A fourth quarter is the fiscal year minus
the first nine months, or minus the three reported quarters when no nine-month figure is tagged. Quarters are calendar
quarters, so a company whose quarter ends a few days into the next month is counted in the quarter it covers.

**Volume and price.** The text of Meta's and Alphabet's 10-Qs is reduced to the sentences and tables that report the
changes, and those are parsed. The pipeline keeps the reduced text and checks on every new filing that it parses
to the same values as the whole document.

**What is kept.** Only the revenue facts of each filing and the reduced text, a small fraction of the filings' size.
The filings themselves stay on EDGAR, a permanent public archive.

</Section>

<Section id="limits" title="Limits">

<ul>
  <li><strong>Definitions differ by company.</strong> Snap, Pinterest and The Trade Desk count total revenue; Snap's includes subscriptions. The Trade Desk's revenue is its fee, not the advertising it buys.</li>
  <li><strong>AppLovin's line changes.</strong> It is the advertising segment until the apps business was sold and total revenue after; one annual figure that mixes the two is flagged and not used.</li>
  <li><strong>The tests are not experiments.</strong> The ATT test has no clean control; the open-web test is dominated by one company's choice.</li>
  <li><strong>Fourth quarters are missing from the volume and price split</strong> because annual reports give only full-year changes.</li>
</ul>
