Laparoscopic surgical robots sit under China's large-medical-equipment licensing regime, which sets a national
and provincial ceiling on how many may be installed. It is the only hard denominator anywhere in this project,
and it comes with two limits that matter more than the number itself.

<Section id="method" title="Method">

Four sources, one of them machine-readable and three of them read by hand.

**openFDA** gives the competitive set and nothing else: not one field in a clearance record relates to volume.
It is structurally convenient here, because the FDA wrote new regulations for the modern entrants between 2024
and 2026 while the incumbent stayed in the legacy code, so the challenger field is {newCodeCount} records
across four new product codes against {legacyCount} in the old one. The De Novo grants that created those
regulations are curated by hand, because openFDA serves 510(k) and has no De Novo endpoint at all.

**Unit figures** come from primary filings: SEC 8-K and 10-K for the American disclosers, HKEXnews interim and
annual reports for the Hong Kong listings, and Shanghai prospectuses for the applicants. Every row records the
company's own counting rule, because five companies here publish a number each calls "units" while counting
five different things, and nothing on this page sums across them.

**Award prices** are read one notice at a time from the Chinese government procurement portal. That portal
searches titles and not bodies, has no interface for machines, rate-limits aggressively and cannot be reached
from outside the country for several provinces, so this is a hand-built price panel and never a shipment
series.

**The quota** is one official table, extracted in full and reconciled against its own national totals.

<Section id="limits" title="What this page cannot tell you">

<ul>
  <li><strong>It cannot give you market share.</strong> No free source states total installed systems
  worldwide, and none states China at all. Every share here is a share of the companies that disclosed, with
  the count of silent ones printed next to it.</li>
  <li><strong>It cannot compare the five unit bases.</strong> Installed base, placements, production, units
  sold and procedures are five different quantities. They are stored apart and never added.</li>
  <li><strong>A placement is not a sale.</strong> Roughly half of Intuitive's placements each quarter are
  operating leases, an increasing share of them usage-based, and the company does not split them.</li>
  <li><strong>Placements are not the change in installed base either.</strong> A fifth to a third of each
  quarter's placements are absorbed by retirements and trade-ins that no filing discloses.</li>
  <li><strong>The award prices are not a price list.</strong> No notice discloses arm count, console count,
  instrument package or warranty, so a cheaper award may simply be a smaller machine.</li>
  <li><strong>Tender coverage is a floor.</strong> Private and military hospitals are absent, as are purchases
  funded outside the government procurement regime.</li>
</ul>
