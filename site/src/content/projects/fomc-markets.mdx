<Section id="method" title="Method">
  <div class="prose">
    <p><strong>Question.</strong> How far ahead, and how accurately, do prediction markets price Federal Reserve decisions, and do the two venues disagree?</p>
    <p><strong>Data.</strong> Every "Fed decision in &lt;month&gt;" event on Polymarket and every <code>KXFEDDECISION</code> event on Kalshi ({num(facts.decision_markets)} markets), plus the rate-level, hike-count and dot-plot markets on both. Daily history is backfilled from Polymarket's price-history endpoint and Kalshi's candlesticks; from September 16, 2026 the snapshot pipeline adds two observations a day including order books.</p>
    <p><strong>Definitions.</strong> Both platforms list the same five outcomes per meeting; they are mapped to one grid (cut 50+, cut 25, hold, hike 25, hike 50+) and rolled up to cut / hold / hike for charts. Kalshi outcomes are keyed on the ticker suffix, because the outcome labels were reworded twice in 2024–25. Probability = order-book mid where quoted, else the displayed price. Expected move = Σ probability × bps. Decision dates follow the Fed's published calendar; 2027 dates are provisional.</p>
    <p><strong>Meetings that closed before tracking began.</strong> The snapshot pipeline started on September 16, 2026 and only sees open markets, so the {facts.archive?.meetings?.length ?? '—'} meetings from {facts.archive?.meetings?.length ? monthYear(`${facts.archive.meetings[0]}-01`) : '—'} to {facts.archive?.meetings?.length ? monthYear(`${facts.archive.meetings.at(-1)}-01`) : '—'} were recovered from each platform's archive: Polymarket's closed events under both of its naming schemes, and Kalshi's settled events through its historical endpoints, which serve anything settled more than about two months ago under different field names. A Kalshi day without a trade uses the bid/ask midpoint when the spread is at most 10 cents; a day missing from Polymarket's daily series is refilled from hourly prices. The recovered meetings feed the price history only; the scorecard still starts with the first meeting the snapshot pipeline saw.</p>
    <p><strong>Price time.</strong> Every daily price is dated by the New York day it was observed. The platforms' own daily history is stamped at the end of the day in UTC terms (Polymarket at midnight UTC, which is the previous evening in New York; Kalshi at midnight New York time, the next UTC date), so those points move back one day; snapshots keep their own time.</p>
    <p><strong>The macro panels.</strong> The target's upper bound, the 2-year yield, the 5-year breakeven and 5-year, 5-year forward inflation rates, and WTI and Brent crude come from FRED; prices and the target are never revised. Jobless claims are revised, so the 4-week average is read from ALFRED's archive of past vintages: each week's first published value, drawn from the day it was published. The priced rate on day D is the target on D + 1 (it changes the day after a decision) plus the two-meeting expected move from D's evening price; a meeting counts only on days all of its outcomes are priced, carried forward at most three days.</p>
    <p><strong>The event study.</strong> Release times come from the BLS and BEA published calendars, not from the Kalshi ladder, whose close stayed at the original time for releases the 2025 shutdown postponed; a release is used only if its ladder was still trading {ev?.window_min?.pre ?? '—'} minutes before. Minute prices come from both platforms (Kalshi: the bid/ask midpoint when the spread is at most 10 cents, otherwise the last trade; Polymarket: its minute price series), carried forward through minutes without a quote; the statistics use {pfName[mainPf]}, chosen on quote coverage before they were computed. The expected move, not the probability of a hike, is measured: through 2025 the hike probability sat near zero at every meeting and its changes measured nothing. Placebo windows are the same clock half hour on weekdays with no CPI, jobs, PCE, PPI, GDP or ECI release, no FOMC decision that day or the day before, and not a Thursday (weekly claims), taking every second such day.</p>
  </div>
</Section>

    <ul>
      <li><strong>Per meeting:</strong> multi-outcome Brier score (sum over the five buckets of squared error; 0 is perfect, 2 is certain and wrong) of each platform's final pre-decision distribution, taken from the last snapshot before 17:30 UTC on decision day (before the snapshot pipeline started: the last daily history point before decision day), plus the probability assigned to the realised outcome.       <li><strong>Cross-platform:</strong> the daily gap in P(hike) and whether it closes into the meeting.</li>
