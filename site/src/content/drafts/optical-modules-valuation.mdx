<Section id="method" title="Method">
  <div class="prose">
    <p><strong>Question.</strong> What is the listed optical-module and optical-component world being priced at right now, how much of that price is the growth analysts expect next year, and who actually has the revenue?</p>
    <p><strong>Pool.</strong> {facts.n_listings} listings across {facts.n_issuers} companies in mainland China, the United States, Japan, Taiwan and Hong Kong. Companies are labelled by how much of them is optical: the specialists, and the diversified groups that sell optical alongside something larger. Diversified groups stay in the valuation table because their multiple is a real comparison point, but the charts draw them hollow and the share table takes their whole revenue only where the whole company competes in this market. Dual-listed issuers appear once per listing, because the whole point is that the same earnings are priced differently in two markets.</p>
    <p><strong>Prices and consensus.</strong> Prices, market caps and non-Chinese analyst consensus come from Yahoo Finance; A-share consensus is built from East Money's per-broker forecasts, taking each house's most recent report, which gives a broker count and a forecast range rather than a single blended number. Trailing earnings come from SEC XBRL for the US listings, East Money's quarterly reports for the A-shares, and Yahoo elsewhere; cumulative filings are differenced into quarters before being summed into a trailing twelve months.</p>
    <p><strong>Definitions.</strong> Trailing PE is market capitalisation over trailing-twelve-month attributable profit, which avoids the share-count conventions that differ across these markets. Forward PE is price over calendar-year consensus EPS, which is adjusted non-GAAP earnings where Yahoo supplies it and 归母净利润 where a Chinese broker does — see the note above the table. Growth is the consensus for {y1} over the consensus for {y0}. Forecast spread is the gap between the highest and lowest broker forecast as a share of the mean. Every ratio converts currencies explicitly at FRED rates, because a listing's price currency, reporting currency and consensus currency can all be different.</p>
    <p><strong>What is deliberately missing.</strong> A negative or absent denominator produces no number and a written reason, never a negative multiple. Companies covered by fewer than three analysts, or by an unpublished number of them, are drawn faded, because a two-broker "consensus" is two opinions.</p>
  </div>
</Section>
